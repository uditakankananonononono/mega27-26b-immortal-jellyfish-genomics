# The five genomes: a field guide for the reader

Every number in the results chapters lives or dies by the quality of the genome it was measured in, so this short chapter profiles the five assemblies as instruments before they are used as evidence.

Turritopsis dohrnii, contig-level assembly GCA_027922465.2. This is the newer and cleaner of the two immortal-jellyfish assemblies: 891 sequences spanning about 436 megabases, consistent with published estimates of the T. dohrnii genome size. For a non-model marine invertebrate this is respectable continuity; genes of ordinary size will usually sit complete on one contig. When a claim in this report matters most, this assembly is the primary source, and its companion serves as replication.

Turritopsis dohrnii, Oviedo scaffold assembly GCA_025167195.1. The assembly associated with the group that produced the reference comparative-genomics study is much more fragmented: 74,835 scaffolds spanning about 400 megabases, an average scaffold of barely five kilobases. Fragmentation at that scale has direct consequences for translated mapping: genes are routinely split across scaffolds, alignments truncate at scaffold ends, and copy-number estimates are depressed. Everywhere the two T. dohrnii assemblies disagree, this asymmetry is the first suspect, and the report says so where it occurs. Its independent provenance still makes it valuable: a locus found in both assemblies is unlikely to be an assembly artefact.

Turritopsis rubra, GCA_039566895.2. The non-reverting congener and the expansion's new comparator is the best assembly in the set: 326 sequences spanning about 267 megabases, chromosome-level. The smaller, cleaner genome is a methodological gift and an analytical hazard at once. Its genes map more completely, so contrasts against T. dohrnii can reflect instrument quality rather than biology; the pre-registered statistics therefore lean on presence and coarse copy classes rather than fine copy counts. Biologically, T. rubra is the comparison the field cares about: same genus, same basic life cycle, no rejuvenation. Whatever distinguishes the immortal species should be visible against this background more clearly than against any other.

Aurelia aurita, GCA_004194415.1. The moon jelly is the distant medusozoan anchor: 2,709 sequences spanning about 377 megabases, carried over from the closed phase. Aurelia is a scyphozoan, separated from the hydrozoan Turritopsis by hundreds of millions of years, so it cannot adjudicate the immortal-versus-mortal contrast within Turritopsis. Its role is calibration and breadth: a gene found in Aurelia and Hydra and Clytia is ordinary cnidarian machinery, and a gene pattern unique to T. dohrnii among these genomes earns a second look precisely because Aurelia stands for the medusozoan mainstream.

Clytia hemisphaerica, GCF_902728285.1. The annotated hydrozoan reference: 1,396 sequences spanning about 421 megabases, with a full RefSeq annotation. Clytia is the calibration genome. Its annotated genes tell us what the mapping pipeline ought to find in a hydrozoan, its RefSeq proteins supply the most sensitive cross-species queries for the jellyfish genomes, and its annotated status makes it the one genome where the pipeline's calls can be checked against independent truth. When the controls chapter says the pipeline behaves, Clytia is where much of that confidence is earned.

One asymmetry deserves plain statement before the results. The immortal species is measured with the two most problematic instruments in the set: a decent contig assembly and a badly fragmented scaffold assembly, while its key comparator, T. rubra, enjoys the best. This stacks the deck against finding extra copies in the immortal species, because fragmentation hides them. Any copy-number advantage that survives that deck-stacking is thereby more interesting, and any absence must be weighed against the possibility that fragmentation, not evolution, did the removing.
