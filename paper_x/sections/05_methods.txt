# Methods

Design overview

The expansion follows a pre-registered, genome-first, calibration-first design. Three commitments shape every step. First, nothing about the panels, hypotheses, thresholds, or statistical tests was changed after outcome data were touched; the analysis plan was committed to the repository before the first query protein was downloaded, and its commit hash is the temporal proof. Second, the mapping pipeline is inherited unchanged from the closed phase, so that new results inherit old calibration rather than freshly tuned judgement. Third, every genome-wide claim is accompanied by a positive control that must reproduce prior calls and a negative control that must stay quiet, and both controls are reported regardless of outcome.

Genomes

Five genome assemblies are analysed. Two are the available Turritopsis dohrnii assemblies: GCA_027922465.2, a contig-level assembly, and GCA_025167195.1, the scaffold-level assembly associated with the Oviedo group; treating both gives an internal replication of every T. dohrnii call across independently produced assemblies. The non-reverting comparators are Turritopsis rubra GCA_039566895.2, a chromosome-level assembly new to this expansion and the phylogenetically closest available mortal contrast, and Aurelia aurita GCA_004194415.1, the moon jelly carried over from the closed phase as a more distantly related medusozoan. Clytia hemisphaerica GCF_902728285.1, a RefSeq-annotated hydrozoan genome, serves as the annotated calibration reference and as a source of cnidarian query proteins. All assemblies were downloaded from NCBI over FTP/HTTPS, and every file's SHA-256 checksum is recorded in the repository's data provenance file together with its URL. A third T. dohrnii accession cited by earlier work, GCA_051903475.1, was inspected and found to expose only assembly reports and statistics, with no sequence files served; it is recorded as unavailable rather than silently dropped.

Query proteins and their retrieval

For each gene in Panels B and C, query proteins were retrieved from NCBI through the E-utilities protein database, following the closed phase's retrieval protocol: an organism-scoped search with the gene symbol in all fields restricted to RefSeq, falling back to a gene-name field search when the first query returns nothing, and selection of the longest RefSeq record among the top candidates, with an alphabetical accession tiebreak so the procedure is deterministic. Human queries come from Homo sapiens. Cnidarian queries come from four annotated species, Clytia hemisphaerica, Hydra vulgaris, Acropora millepora, and Nematostella vectensis, because cross-species query sets raise sensitivity for deep homologs, exactly as the closed phase demonstrated when Clytia queries produced the strongest jellyfish-genome matches. Every retrieved accession and sequence length is logged in a manifest; genes for which no cnidarian query exists are mapped with the human query alone, and that asymmetry is visible in the manifest rather than hidden.

The mapping pipeline, frozen

Mapping uses tBLASTn 2.17.0 against per-genome BLAST databases built with makeblastdb. Search parameters are the closed phase's, unchanged: e-value threshold 1e-5, at most twenty target sequences per query, tabular output with query and subject coordinates and lengths. Query coverage is computed per high-scoring pair as the covered fraction of the query length. Hits for the same gene across all query species are merged and clustered into loci: high-scoring pairs on the same contig within fifty kilobases are grouped, each locus carries the sum of its members' bit scores, and a locus is called strong when the summed score reaches one hundred bits, the threshold frozen by the closed phase. For each gene and genome the ledger records the number of loci, the number of strong loci, and the best locus with its identity, coverage, strand, and coordinates. A gene is called present in a genome when it has at least one strong locus; its copy signal is the count of strong loci. This is a homology-mapping method, not a gene-prediction method: it establishes that sequence recognisably homologous to the query exists at a locus, and it does not attempt to build exon models, which is a separate and harder problem deliberately left out of scope.

Controls

Two controls gate interpretation, as pre-registered. The positive control re-runs the closed phase's Panel A queries against the rebuilt databases and requires the presence calls of the closed phase's ledger to reproduce; any mismatch is treated as a pipeline defect and blocks interpretation of the new panels until resolved. The negative control shuffles every Panel B and C query protein with a fixed seed, preserving length and overall composition while destroying homology, and runs the shuffled set against every genome through the identical pipeline. The false-positive rate is the fraction of shuffled queries producing a strong locus, and the pre-registered validity criterion is five percent or less in every genome. If the negative control fails, the run is invalid and only the control may be used to diagnose why; the outcome data may not be re-tuned.

Statistical protocol

Hypotheses H1 through H6 were fixed in the preregistration. H1 and H2 test whether at least half of Panel B and Panel C, respectively, map in the T. dohrnii assemblies, using a one-sided binomial test against the negative-control false-positive rate, with a small-count correction so a zero observed control rate does not produce a degenerate test. H3 tests the copy-number contrast per gene between the immortal species and the mortal comparators with Fisher exact tests, Benjamini-Hochberg corrected across the panel; the pre-registered reading is that surviving contrasts nominate candidate expansion genes, while no survivors is an honest negative for the expansion hypothesis in this panel. H4 tests whether the presence map separates the rejuvenating species from the non-reverting cnidarians, using the difference between within-Turritopsis-dohrnii and cross-species mean Jaccard distance as the statistic and an exact permutation over genome labels for the null; the known caveat, stated in the preregistration and repeated here, is that the two T. dohrnii entries are assemblies of one species, so within-group similarity partly reflects assembly redundancy, and H4 is interpreted with that limitation. H5 tests whether the genes mapping in T. dohrnii from Panels B and C overlap more than expected under independence, by Fisher exact test over the union gene universe. H6 is the controls hypothesis above. Benjamini-Hochberg correction is applied within each pre-registered family at q below 0.05, and every test, significant or not, appears in the results. Exploratory analyses are labelled as such and never upgrade a pre-registered negative.

Implementation and reproducibility

All code is in the repository: the panel definitions in jellyfish/xpanel.py, the statistics in jellyfish/xstats.py with unit tests for every function, the fetch, mapping, and analysis drivers in experiments/. The test suite, which included forty-one tests at the unification commit, grows with the expansion; every new module ships with tests, including synthetic-case tests for the statistics and frozen-list tests for the panels. The full pipeline runs on a two-core, one-gigabyte sandbox, which is itself a small methodological point: none of this requires a cluster, and every number in the report can be regenerated from the repository, the recorded checksums, and public data.

Page-count methodology for this report

The standing format rule for this program is fifty or more pages of body text, with headings, references, appendices, and diagrams excluded from the count, and diagrams explicitly not counting toward the total. The count in this report is computed mechanically: the body prose (chapters one through twelve, excluding headings, figure captions, the reference list, and appendices) is assembled as a standalone document, typeset at twelve-point with one-and-a-half line spacing, rendered to PDF, and its page count read from the rendered file. The formula, the body word count, and the rendered page count are reported in the colophon so the compliance number is checkable rather than asserted.
