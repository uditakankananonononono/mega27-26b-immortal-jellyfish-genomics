APP (Alzheimer). Amyloid precursor protein is the substrate of the Alzheimer cascade: sequential cleavage by beta- and gamma-secretase releases amyloid-beta, whose aggregation defines the disease's plaques. APP's normal function involves synaptic adhesion and signalling, functions old enough that homologs exist across animals, including in species with no amyloid pathology; the disease is a derived malfunction layered on an ancient protein. MAPT (tauopathy). MAPT encodes tau, the microtubule-associated protein whose aggregation defines tauopathies including frontotemporal dementia and the tangles of Alzheimer disease. Tau's normal role in axonal transport is tied to long axons and complex neural architectures, so its phylogenetic distribution is expected to track nervous-system elaboration; a cnidarian absence would be expected and informative. NOT MAPPED: no strong locus for MAPT in either T. dohrnii assembly at the frozen threshold. Comparator status: absent in T. rubra, absent in A. aurita.

SNCA (Parkinson). Alpha-synuclein is the aggregating protein of Lewy bodies in Parkinson disease and multiple system atrophy. Its normal function at synaptic vesicle pools is vertebrate-flavoured, and clear orthologs outside vertebrates are contested, making it a test case for how far back synaptic maintenance genes go. NOT MAPPED: no strong locus for SNCA in either T. dohrnii assembly at the frozen threshold. Comparator status: absent in T. rubra, absent in A. aurita.

PRNP (prion). The prion protein is the substrate of Creutzfeldt-Jakob and related transmissible spongiform diseases. Its normal function remains debated, involving copper handling and synaptic signalling. PRNP is a vertebrate gene; its expected absence in cnidarians marks prion disease as a vertebrate-specific vulnerability. GBA (LSD/Parkinson). GBA encodes glucocerebrosidase, the lysosomal enzyme deficient in Gaucher disease; carriers of GBA mutations have markedly elevated Parkinson risk, tying lysosomal storage biology to synucleinopathy. Lysosomal enzymes are ancient, and GBA's mapping tests the depth of the lysosome-neurodegeneration axis. NOT MAPPED: no strong locus for GBA in either T. dohrnii assembly at the frozen threshold. BDNF (neurotrophin). Brain-derived neurotrophic factor is the most studied neurotrophin, sustaining neuronal survival, synaptic plasticity, and memory; its decline tracks cognitive aging, and its supplementation is a perennial therapeutic hope. Neurotrophin-family signalling has cnidarian precursors, so BDNF's mapping tests how old trophic support of neurons really is. NOT MAPPED: no strong locus for BDNF in either T. dohrnii assembly at the frozen threshold. Comparator status: absent in T. rubra, absent in A. aurita.

NGF (neurotrophin). Nerve growth factor, the founding neurotrophin, sustains cholinergic and sensory neurons and is lost early in Alzheimer-relevant circuits. Like BDNF it belongs to a family with deep metazoan precursors, and the pair jointly test the antiquity of trophic neuronal maintenance. NOT MAPPED: no strong locus for NGF in either T. dohrnii assembly at the frozen threshold. Comparator status: absent in T. rubra, absent in A. aurita.
