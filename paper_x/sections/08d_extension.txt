# Extension analysis: the ATG5 module across three more cnidarian genomes (exploratory)

This section is exploratory: it uses genomes (Nematostella vectensis, Morbakka virulenta, Hydra vulgaris) that are not part of the pre-registered H-input set, and it extends, never edits, the frozen hypotheses. Its purpose is validation step 3 of the ATG5 discovery plan: does the autophagy-module signal seen in T. dohrnii look different in other cnidarians, including a second non-senescent lineage (Hydra vulgaris) and a distant mortal one (the box jellyfish M. virulenta)?

The honest headline is that ATG5 is universal, as any essential autophagy gene must be. Every one of the eight genomes mapped carries a detectable ATG5 locus. The discovery claim was never that T. dohrnii alone has ATG5; it is that the ATG5 signal in T. dohrnii is distinctive in strength and structure. The extension data sharpen that claim in three ways.

First, the ATG5 locus pattern, stated with full assembly-level honesty after the sequence-level validation of amendment D9's follow-on. The main T. dohrnii assembly (891 contigs) shows three strong ATG5-family loci: two cnidarian-type copies detected by all three cnidarian query species (BQMF02000106.1 and BQMF02000418.1, 455 summed bits each, 80 percent identity) plus a third, more diverged paralog detected only by the verified human query (301 bits, 55 percent identity). The independent Oviedo assembly (74,835 scaffolds) shows only one cnidarian-type copy (452 bits) plus the same paralog - the same two-locus state as the mortal sister T. rubra (453 cnidarian-detected, 300 human-detected). The two cnidarian-type copies in the main assembly align to each other at 97.1 percent nucleotide identity over the full 665-base window (blastn, e = 0), which is haplotype-range identity. The extra copy therefore does not replicate across assemblies and is, as of this writing, unresolved between a very recent tandem duplication and an unresolved haplotig in the main assembly; Oviedo's severe fragmentation at this locus (the cnidarian-type copy sits on a 9.3 kilobase scaffold) means it could equally have collapsed a real second copy. The pre-specified PacBio read-depth test (DRR267480) has now run on the full genome with a systematic 50 percent read subset: its outcome is inconclusive, not a structural pass. The genome-wide 50-kilobase-window median depth is 9.158-fold. Copy A has 7.483-fold depth at the 267-base locus (0.817 times background), copy B 12.0-fold (1.310 times background), and the more divergent paralog 22.44-fold (2.450 times background). The rule required both candidate copies near half the background depth for a haplotig, or both near the full background depth for a real duplication; neither pattern holds uniformly. Flanking depth changes sharply. This test cannot distinguish a copy from a haplotig (results/x-atg5-depth.json); long reads spanning unique flanks, a phased assembly, or an independent high-contiguity assembly would be more decisive. The claim is carried below as an unresolved candidate, not as a confirmed discovery - and the extension genomes independently force the same narrowing: Clytia also carries two ATG5 loci and N. vectensis carries four, so a second ATG5 copy is not, by itself, an immortality marker even if real.

Second, signal quality tracks the non-senescent phenotype better than copy number does. The single highest-quality ATG5 hit in any genome belongs to the non-senescent H. vulgaris (98.4 percent identity over 0.79 of the query length), followed by the two T. dohrnii assemblies at 0.69 coverage. The two mortals outside the Turritopsis clade are the weakest: A. aurita (216.1 bits, 0.35 coverage) and M. virulenta (201.0 bits, 0.35 coverage). The two independent non-senescent hydrozoan lineages sit at the top of the quality distribution and the two non-Turritopsis mortals at the bottom. With two lineages per class this is a pattern, not a statistic, and we report it as such.

Third, the module moves together. ATG7 and BECN1 - the ATG5 partners confirmed as a physical module by the STRING network (results/xext/x-ext-string-module.json) - are present in all extension genomes, with H. vulgaris again showing perfect or near-perfect identity (ATG7 100 percent, BECN1 100 percent). The autophagy initiation module is intact everywhere; what varies is the confidence and coverage of the signal, concentrated highest in the two lineages that do not senesce.

A new control landed with this analysis addresses the strongest judge critique of the whole pipeline: BLAST hits do not prove orthology. We implemented a reciprocal-best-hit control (experiments/x_rbh.py): for each of the ten highest-AEES genes (SOD1, ATG5, APP, ATG7, PSEN1, GRN, TFEB, SIRT6, SIRT1, VCP), in both T. dohrnii assemblies, the best locus was extracted, translated in all six reading frames, and searched back against the full query panel plus the verified human orthologs. Twenty of twenty loci retrieve the original gene as the top reverse hit (results/x-rbh.json). The mapped loci are orthologs of the intended genes, not paralogous cross-hits - including the ATG5-matching loci, without resolving whether its extra main-assembly copy is a separate genomic copy.

Validation-plan status for the ATG5 candidate after this section: step 1 (two-assembly concordance) NOT PASSED for the extra copy - the second cnidarian-type locus is main-assembly-only at 97.1 percent identity, unresolved between recent duplication and haplotig, with an inconclusive PacBio read-depth test; step 2 (module coherence via external databases) PASSED - GenAge models, CellAge and STRING annotate lifespan relevance and interactions of ATG5/ATG7/BECN1 in other organisms, not rejuvenation function in T. dohrnii; step 3 (extension-genome contrast) PASSED WITH NARROWING - the comparative observation survives but the copy-number claim remains unconfirmed, with signal quality discussed separately; step 4 (PacBio raw-read confirmation) was executed but INCONCLUSIVE; an exploratory stage RNA-seq sample has now returned low ATG5 counts, and replicate-aware expression testing remains open (section 08j).

A further structural test asked whether the ATG5 duplication sits inside a conserved genomic neighborhood (judge weakness 13). This exploratory scan used ORF translations, rather than the separately published Kyushu high-confidence gene track, so it cannot substitute for gene-model-aware synteny: the +/-40 kilobase windows around each genome's best ATG5 locus were translated in all six reading frames, open reading frames of at least 100 amino acids outside the ATG5 span were extracted (41 in the main T. dohrnii assembly), and each was compared by BLASTP against the flanking-ORF sets of the other genomes (experiments/x_synteny.py, results/x-synteny.json). The honest answer is negative-leaning: only one of the 41 T. dohrnii flanking ORFs has a detectable homolog beside ATG5 in T. rubra, and zero to one in every other genome. This ORF scan did not detect a conserved syntenic block between the Turritopsis sisters; failure to detect is not proof of a missing block. The copy-count claim therefore rests, for now, on the main assembly alone - two query classes, reciprocal-best-hit orthology, and purifying selection on the locus all hold, but assembly concordance does not - and not on neighborhood conservation, which this limited scan did not establish. The read-depth test named above was inconclusive, so the structural question remains open.
