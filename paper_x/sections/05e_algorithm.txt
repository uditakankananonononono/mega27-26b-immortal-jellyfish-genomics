# Genome-first annotation recovery as a bounded workflow

GFAR names a set of steps: verified panel queries, translated alignment to assemblies, fixed 50 kb clustering and 100-bit strong-locus cutoff, assembly-aware evidence scoring, controls, and negative-result reporting. The executable tBLASTn arm uses e-value 1e-5 and 50 kb clustering (experiments/x_map.py; jellyfish/loci.py). These are *mapping* thresholds, not an orthogroup or gene-prediction algorithm. The independent Kyushu project already produced 23,314 high-confidence models and ran OrthoFinder; OrthoFinder itself infers orthogroups and duplication events, and CAFE models gene-family-count error (Hasegawa et al. 2023; Emms and Kelly 2019; Han et al. 2013). Our workflow should not be framed as first annotation or proven algorithmic novelty.

A paired calibration on Clytia holds mapping output and cutoff fixed: cnidarian-plus-human queries recover strong loci for 35 of 47 genes versus 27 of 47 for human-only queries (results/x-benchmark-recall.json). This tests query-selection sensitivity, not precision or discovery superiority against published protein models and orthogroup pipelines. A separate NCBI organism/gene-field lookup returned no entries for 47 target labels, but did not search the provider's gene catalog; it is a database-route comparison only. Exact panel-wide gene-family duplication, transcriptomic effect and functional mechanism remain separate questions requiring orthogonal evidence. See docs/X-ALGORITHM.md for the historical formulation and its now-qualified benchmark.
