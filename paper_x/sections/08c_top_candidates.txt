The archetype this project follows demands a named top candidate with a falsifiable validation plan rather than statistics alone. The candidate ranking uses the frozen AEES scores restricted to genes detected in BOTH T. dohrnii assemblies (concordance 1.0), which is a stronger detection class than one-assembly hits, but not proof of biological function.

Lead candidate: ATG5 (autophagy-related 5). AEES 0.860, second-highest in the panel behind SOD1 at 0.862 (the two are separated by less than the score's noise floor, and ATG5 holds rank 2 under both frozen and equal weights); detected in both T. dohrnii assemblies; three strong loci in the main assembly (two cnidarian-type copies and one human-detected paralog) - but the second cnidarian-type copy is not reproduced in the independent Oviedo assembly, the two main-assembly copies are 97.1 percent identical, and the extra copy is therefore carried as an unresolved candidate (recent duplication versus assembly haplotig) after inconclusive PacBio depth (copy A 0.817x, copy B 1.310x genome-wide median) and a full-read unique-flank test with zero reads spanning both 5 kb flanks at either locus, not as a confirmed duplication; best locus 455 bits at 80 percent identity covering 69 percent of the query, the highest domain-integrity score among top candidates. Autophagy is the core cellular self-renewal machinery: it clears damaged proteins and organelles, and its upregulation extends healthspan in model organisms. A jellyfish that reverts its life cycle is, mechanistically, a jellyfish that re-specifies and rebuilds its cells, and autophagy is a plausible process to test during that rebuild. ATG5 sits at the essential core of the pathway (the ATG12-ATG5 conjugation system) and in the pre-repair analysis it was one of the genes the human-only query arm would have missed in calibration - the cnidarian queries rescued it.

Supporting candidates: SIRT1 and SIRT6 (AEES 0.792 and 0.795, both assemblies, 7 and 3 strong loci respectively), the two sirtuins most consistently tied to lifespan regulation and genome maintenance in mammals; and GRN (progranulin, AEES 0.786), whose best locus is the single strongest alignment in the entire expansion run (8299 total bits), linking a frontotemporal-dementia gene to the neuronal-maintenance side of the hypothesis.

The falsifiable validation plan for ATG5 has four preregistered-style steps, each with a kill condition. One, sequence validation: extract the two T. dohrnii ATG5 loci (main-assembly coordinates in results/x-ledger-bc.json), predict the gene model, translate, and require reciprocal-best-hit status against metazoan ATG5 plus the ATG5-characteristic domain architecture; the candidate dies if the loci do not reconstruct a coherent ATG5 protein. Two, expression evidence: search public T. dohrnii transcriptome and SRA resources for ATG5-derived reads, including the published life-cycle-reversal RNA-seq; the claim "present" upgrades to "present and transcribed" only with read support, and differential expression across the reversal stages is the key test - the rejuvenation hypothesis predicts upregulation during transdifferentiation, and failure to find stage-dependent expression downgrades the candidate to genomic presence only. Three, contrast extension: map ATG5 in the three extension assemblies (N. vectensis, M. virulenta, H. vulgaris) under the identical frozen pipeline; the discovery claim narrows if non-reverting cnidarians show equivalent loci with equivalent confidence. Four, panel placement: test whether autophagy-pathway neighbors (ATG7, BECN1, both already mapped at high confidence in both assemblies) form a coherent module in T. dohrnii; a coordinated autophagy module is a stronger claim than any single gene and is independently falsifiable per member.

What is claimed today is deliberately narrower than what the validation plan would establish: ATG5 is present in the T. dohrnii genome at the highest evidence grade this pipeline can assign, it sits in the pathway most mechanistically tied to cellular rejuvenation, and it belongs to the gene class invisible to a human-only design. The expression and module tests decide whether it becomes a biological claim.
