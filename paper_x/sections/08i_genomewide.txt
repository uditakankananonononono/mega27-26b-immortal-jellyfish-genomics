# The unbiased genome-wide arm: all of GenAge through the same pipeline

Judge weakness 16 asked for a panel-free discovery arm: if the pipeline only ever sees the genes we chose to look for, every enrichment claim is circular. We therefore fetched the complete GenAge human compendium - 307 genes, of which 302 were retrieved and exact-gene verified through the same UniProt route as the repaired panel (five had no reviewed human match and are disclosed in data/xpanel_genage/verify_report.json) - and mapped all 302 through the frozen pipeline on the T. dohrnii main assembly (experiments/x_map.py genage mode, results/x-ledger-genage.json). Two hundred thirty-one genes produce ledger entries, 188 with at least one strong locus, and every gene was scored with the same frozen AEES weights, with the concordance component held at its single-assembly value pending the Oviedo rerun (experiments/x_genage_rank.py, results/x-genage-aees.json).

The first result is a honesty check on the score itself. The top of the genome-wide ranking is dominated by ancient, ubiquitously conserved machinery - ubiquitin B (0.875), RAD51 (0.846), HDAC3 (0.817), HSPA8 (0.809), EEF1A1, HSP90AA1, MT-CO1 - exactly the genes any BLAST-evidence score should rank highest in any animal genome, because their long, slowly evolving proteins produce the strongest alignments. AEES behaves as designed, and that behavior is why the curated panel is not enriched at the top of the whole-GenAge ranking (Mann-Whitney p = 0.35; five panel genes in the top sixty). We report this plainly: the panel's value was never that its members out-align housekeeping genes, but that it connects the longevity and neurology literature; the genome-wide arm confirms the score measures conservation evidence, not panel membership.

The second result is the discovery yield the amendment asked for. Ranking high among GenAge genes the panel never included are RAD51 (0.846), the homologous-recombination repair factor; HDAC3 (0.817) and HDAC1/HDAC2, the chromatin-maintenance deacetylases; CHEK2 (0.71), the DNA-damage checkpoint kinase; SERPINE1 (0.727), the canonical senescence marker PAI-1; and SOD2 (0.68), the mitochondrial superoxide dismutase - which pairs with the panel's rank-one candidate SOD1 to put both cellular superoxide dismutases among the highest-evidence loci in the immortal jellyfish. These are not panel-chosen narratives; they are what an unbiased sweep of the aging-gene universe returns when the frozen pipeline is pointed at T. dohrnii, and they converge on the same modules the panel analysis flagged: genome maintenance, chromatin control, and redox defense. The Oviedo concordance rerun for this arm is logged as pending work, and no genome-wide candidate is claimed above tier 2 until it lands.
