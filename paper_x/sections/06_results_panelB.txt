FOXO3 (stress/longevity-TF). FOXO3 is the most consistently replicated longevity-associated gene in human populations; protective alleles enrich in centenarians across ancestries. MTOR (nutrient-sensing). IGF1 (insulin/IGF). Insulin-like growth factor 1 is the effector of the growth-hormone axis. Circulating IGF1 declines with age, but reduced IGF1 signalling extends lifespan in model organisms, a paradox resolved by distinguishing development-time growth promotion from adult maintenance suppression. Small, long-lived dog breeds and long-lived human kindreds sit at the low-signalling end. NOT MAPPED: no strong locus for IGF1 in either T. dohrnii assembly at the frozen threshold. CDKN2A (senescence). CDKN2A encodes p16INK4a, the senescence brake whose expression rises steeply with age and serves as a standard biomarker of senescent cell burden. Clearing p16-positive cells extends healthspan in mice. Its evolutionary distribution is uneven: it is a relatively recent vertebrate innovation, so its absence from a cnidarian genome would be the expected, informative result. APOE (lipid/neuro). Apolipoprotein E is the strongest common genetic factor for both human longevity and Alzheimer disease: the e4 allele raises Alzheimer risk and shortens lifespan, e2 does the reverse. APOE is a vertebrate lipid-transport gene, expected to be absent from cnidarians; its role in this study is to mark, by contrast, which parts of the aging-neurology interface are evolutionary novelties. NOT MAPPED: no strong locus for APOE in either T. dohrnii assembly at the frozen threshold. Comparator status: absent in T. rubra, absent in A. aurita.
