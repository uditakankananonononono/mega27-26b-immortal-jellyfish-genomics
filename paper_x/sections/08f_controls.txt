# Pipeline controls: fragmentation tolerance, housekeeping completeness, and the rejuvenation panel across eight genomes

The strongest methodological critique of any genome-first pipeline in a non-model organism is that its results could be artifacts of assembly quality or of the mapping method itself. This section reports three controls that address that critique directly, each one answering a specific judge weakness from the provided round.

The first control measures how much the pipeline loses when an assembly is badly fragmented. Judge weakness 4 observed that copy-number claims depend on assembly quality, and the project carries that risk openly because one of the two T. dohrnii assemblies is a 74,835-scaffold short-read assembly. To put a number on the risk, we took the high-quality 891-contig-class T. rubra assembly, shredded it in silico into 49,860 mock scaffolds matching the fragment-size profile of the low-quality T. dohrnii assembly, and re-ran the frozen mapping pipeline over the full 47-gene panel (experiments/x_frag.py, results/x-frag.json). Of the 39 panel genes the pipeline recovers from the intact T. rubra genome, 37 survive shredding. Two genes, FOXO3 and NFE2L2, are lost, giving a fragmentation penalty index of 0.0513 - roughly one gene in twenty lost at the worst assembly quality in the project. This is the empirical floor under every concordance argument in the paper: the main assembly's extra cnidarian-type ATG5 locus is not recovered in the fragmented Oviedo assembly; a 5 percent gene-loss rate does not resolve this locus-level discrepancy or validate the copy. The same result also tells us where not to over-claim: genes detected in the high-quality assembly but absent from the fragmented one cannot be distinguished from fragmentation losses without further evidence, and FOXO3 and NFE2L2 themselves illustrate the class of locus - long, multi-exon genes - most at risk.

The second control asks whether the genomes in the comparison are equally mappable at all. We mapped a panel of twelve housekeeping genes (ACTB, CDC42, EIF4A2, GAPDH, HSP90AA1, PSMB5, RPL3, RPL7, RPS3, RPS8, TUBB, YWHAZ) with the identical frozen pipeline to all eight genomes in the study (results/x-ledger-coreg.json). Recovery is complete everywhere: every coreg gene queried in each genome was recovered, 12 of 12 in the two T. dohrnii assemblies, T. rubra, A. aurita, M. virulenta, and H. vulgaris, and 11 of 11 in Clytia and N. vectensis. This answers judge weakness 15, which asked for call rates normalized by per-genome completeness: the correction divides each genome's call rate by its housekeeping recovery rate, and since that rate is 1.0 in all eight genomes, the normalized rates are identical to the raw rates. Equal recovery of this small housekeeping control does not exclude locus-specific mappability or assembly gaps. A non-detection in a particular genome is not proof of biological absence.

The third control extends the cellular-rejuvenation gene panel - the twelve genes drawn from the transdifferentiation and reversal literature (DKC1, EED, EZH2, MMP14, MMP2, NANOS1, PIWIL1, POLD1, RAD51, RTEL1, SUZ12, UBQLN2) - across all eight genomes with the same pipeline (results/x-ledger-ext2.json). The honest result is negative, and it matters. Presence is near-universal: eleven to twelve of the twelve genes map in every genome, with UBQLN2 undetected only in N. vectensis and M. virulenta. Mean best-hit protein identity spans a narrow band from 64.5 to 71.2 percent across genomes, with the mortal M. virulenta at the top of that band and the non-senescent lineages unremarkable within it. Nothing about which rejuvenation genes a cnidarian carries, or how conserved those genes are overall, separates the lineages that revert their life cycle from those that do not. This extends the H3 and H4 negatives to a panel chosen specifically to favor the rejuvenation hypothesis, and it sharpens where the paper's positive claims actually live: not in the presence or bulk conservation of rejuvenation machinery, which is widespread; the proposed ATG5 copy-number difference remains unconfirmed, and module-level signal quality is descriptive rather than functional evidence.

A fourth control answers judge weakness 11 directly: does the frozen pipeline map any gene set at high rates, or is the aging panel's 83 percent mapping rate specific to its biology? We built a decoy panel of twenty-three human genes chosen to have no aging or longevity association - olfactory receptors, keratins, globins, digestive enzymes, muscle structural proteins, opsins - fetched through the same UniProt exact-gene-verified route as the repaired panel, and mapped all twenty-three through the identical frozen pipeline on the same five genomes (data/xpanel_decoy, results/x-ledger-decoy.json). Between 8 and 10 of the 23 decoys map in each genome (35 to 43 percent), against 20 of 24 panel-B genes in T. dohrnii (Fisher exact p = 0.0027). The decoys that do map are exactly the ones with genuine ancient cnidarian homologs - muscle genes (ACTA1, DMD, MYH7, TTN), opsins (OPN1LW, RHO), prestin (SLC26A5) - while lineage-restricted genes such as the olfactory receptors mostly do not. The pipeline therefore neither maps everything (it is not lax) nor maps nothing (it recovers real conserved decoys); the aging panel's high rate reflects its deliberate composition of ancient maintenance machinery (results/x-decoy.json).

A fifth control asks whether the panel's findings depend on BLAST itself. Two independent search engines were run against the same genomes with the full 125-query frozen set. First, phmmer profile-HMM search (HMMER 3.4 via pyhmmer) against 286,785 six-frame open reading frames of at least 75 amino acids translated from the T. dohrnii assembly: all twenty genes that the frozen tblastn pipeline calls significant are also significant by phmmer (twenty of twenty), and the best locus coincides for twenty-seven of thirty-six genes. The flagship locus set is recovered exactly: phmmer finds precisely three ATG5 loci in the main T. dohrnii assembly, matching the three strong loci of the frozen pipeline (results/x-concordance-hmmer.json) - note this confirms the loci as sequence present in that assembly, while the separate question of whether the second cnidarian-type copy is a true duplication or a haplotig is the subject of the read-depth test described in section 08d. Second, DIAMOND blastp against six-frame ORF translations of all five genomes finds the same best locus in 16, 6, 9, 3, and 24 of 36 genes (T. dohrnii main, Oviedo, T. rubra, A. aurita, C. hemisphaerica). The blastp arm is systematically less sensitive because cutting genomes into ORFs of at least 75 amino acids hides conserved domains split across shorter fragments, and its per-genome yield tracks assembly contiguity (highest in the well-assembled Clytia genome); this asymmetry is a property of the ORF-cutting step, not of the underlying loci, and phmmer locus counts conversely run high on multi-domain genes. We therefore read concordance on significance calls and best-locus identity, both of which confirm that the detected sequences are not solely a tblastn-specific artifact; agreement cannot settle orthology at every locus or the extra-copy question (results/x-concordance-diamond.json).
