# The provided judge verdict and what it changed

After the first complete build, the project owner provided a formal judge round: a twenty-point critique (archived verbatim with provenance in docs/X-JUDGE-ROUNDS.md) whose demands were locked as an amendment queue (docs/X-AMENDMENTS-P1.md) before any new execution. This section reports the amendments that have already landed; the rest proceed in the open work program.

Ranking robustness, demanded as weakness 7, is now measured rather than asserted. The AEES weights (bitscore 0.35, query coverage 0.25, identity 0.15, assembly concordance 0.25) were frozen before scoring, but the judge is right that freezing is not the same as robustness. We therefore recomputed the T. dohrnii ranking under equal weights and under 200 random weightings: ATG5 holds rank 1 under both frozen and equal weights, the frozen top five (ATG5, APP, ATG7, PSEN1, GHR) retains a mean 3.71 of 5 members under random weightings, and ATG5's rank under adversarial weightings spans 1 to 27. The honest summary: the top of the ranking is robust to reasonable weight choices, and we disclose the weightings under which it is not.

The enrichment framework demanded as weakness 17 returns an honest negative. Testing whether the twenty high-AEES genes (score 0.75 or above, of 44 detected) are enriched for membership in GenAge human, GenAge model organisms, CellAge, or LongevityMap gives no significant enrichment on any database (Fisher exact, Benjamini-Hochberg q between 0.76 and 0.77). The confound is structural: the panel was selected from aging biology, so testing within-panel enrichment against aging databases is close to circular. The non-circular version of this test is the unbiased genome-wide discovery arm (weakness 16), which is part of the open work program: there, known aging pathways must emerge from a panel-free scan rather than being selected in advance.

Framing discipline, weakness 8, is adopted as a rule of the text: this paper claims evolutionary conservation of cellular maintenance pathways, never therapeutic implication. The neurology and aging connections argued throughout are statements about shared maintenance machinery and about where maintenance biology is concentrated in a rejuvenating animal - not claims that a jellyfish gene treats a human disease.
