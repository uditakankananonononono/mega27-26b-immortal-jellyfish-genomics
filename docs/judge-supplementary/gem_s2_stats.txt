1. Pipeline & Homology Detection Flaws

The Flaw (tBLASTn Reliance): Using a frozen tBLASTn pipeline across deep evolutionary distances (Hydrozoans vs. Scyphozoans like Aurelia aurita) will choke on divergent sequences, pseudogenes, and missing annotations. tBLASTn cannot distinguish true orthologs from fast-evolving paralogs or fragmented assembly artifacts.

Fix to Improve & Innovate: Replace tBLASTn with an orthology-aware, reciprocal best-hit pipeline using Orthofinder or SonicParanoid, combined with profile Hidden Markov Models (HMMER3). To make it genuinely novel, run structural homology mapping using Foldseek on AlphaFold2/ESMFold predictions to catch structurally conserved longevity proteins that have drifted beyond sequence recognition.

2. Weak Gene Selection & Gene Families

The Flaw (Human-Centric Bias): Selection of 24 human/mammalian aging and neurodegeneration genes ignores cnidarian biology. Cnidarians lack complex central nervous systems, classical neurodegenerative pathways (e.g., specific mammalian synucleinopathies), and mammalian-specific signaling hubs.

Fix to Improve & Innovate: Expand your candidate gene set to include ancestral gene families explicitly tied to stemness, transdifferentiation, and cellular rejuvenation (e.g., PIWI/piRNA path, FoxO/Myc networks, heat shock protein (HSP) expansions, and DNA damage repair machinery like PARP/RAD51). Add functional domain architecture mapping (InterProScan) to demonstrate functional domain gains/losses rather than binary presence/absence.

3. Flawed Statistical Tests & Multiple-Testing Boundaries

The Flaw (Fisher's Exact & Independent Label Permutation):

Fisher’s Exact Test per-gene assumes gene copy numbers are independent observations. They are heavily confounded by underlying genome duplication events and assembly completeness differences.

The Single Most Novel Analysis You Should Add

Lineage-Specific Positive Selection Analysis (dN/dS) Integrated with Transdifferentiation-Stage Expression Data.

Instead of just counting presence, absence, or copy number, test whether key longevity genes undergo adaptive evolution specifically on the Turritopsis dohrnii branch.

Run PAML (CodeML) or HyPhy (BS-REL / MEME) on multi-species ortholog alignments to detect branches and specific codon sites under positive selection (ω=dN/dS>1) along the T. dohrnii lineage compared to T. rubra and other cnidarians.

Cross-reference these positively selected genes with publicly available RNA-seq datasets spanning the life-cycle reversion process (medusa → cyst → polyp) to demonstrate that adaptively evolved genes are dynamically regulated during cellular rejuvenation.
