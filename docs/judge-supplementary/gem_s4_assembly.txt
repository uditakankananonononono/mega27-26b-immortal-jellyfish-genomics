The Fatal Flaw: Assembly Asymmetry and Gene Loss Artifacts

You are comparing a hyper-fragmented 74,835-scaffold draft (T. dohrnii) against a highly contiguous, chromosome-level reference (T. rubra at 326 scaffolds).

In genomics, fragmentation masquerades as gene loss, truncation, and copy-number variation.

When your T. dohrnii draft has tens of thousands of gaps and scaffold breaks:

False Negatives (Absence): A gene reported as "absent" in T. dohrnii is very likely sitting on an unassembled read, trapped in a gap, or split across two unlinked contigs where your tBLASTn threshold failed to capture the fragmented hit.

False Positives (Copy Number Drops): You cannot reliably claim gene family contractions or expansions when one genome's assembly graph collapsed duplicated paralogs due to low coverage or short-read resolution, while the chromosome-level reference resolved every single tandem duplication.

Using a frozen tBLASTn pipeline across genomes with a 200-fold difference in scaffold count renders your presence/absence contrasts scientifically untrustworthy.

How to Rescue the Project and Make It Novel

To convince me (and the grand award jury) that your differential gene counts aren't just assembly artifacts, you need to implement the following controls and novel analytical frameworks:

1. Quantify and Benchmark Gene Complete-ness First

What to add: Run BUSCO (Benchmarking Universal Single-Copy Orthologs) using the Metazoa or Cnidaria lineage datasets across all four assemblies.

The Control: Establish a baseline "assembly completeness matrix." If T. dohrnii is missing 15% of universal single-copy core metazoan genes due to assembly gaps, you must apply a statistical correction or penalty factor to any claimed gene loss in your 24 aging/neurodegeneration target set.

2. Shift from tBLASTn to Raw Read Mapping (Coverage-Based Copy Number Estimation)

What to change: Stop relying solely on assembled contigs/scaffold sequences for copy-number variation.

The Novel Rescue Analysis: Download the raw short-read sequencing data (SRA runs) for T. dohrnii. Map the raw reads directly to the high-quality T. rubra coding sequences (CDS) or conserved domains using BWA-MEM or Bowtie2. Calculate depth-of-coverage across conserved exonic regions normalized by median genome coverage.

Why this works: Even if T. dohrnii contigs failed to assemble, raw reads don't lie. If the raw read depth over a specific longevity gene domain matches the genome-wide median coverage, the gene is present—regardless of scaffold fragmentation.

3. Synteny and Flanking Sequence Verification for Claimed "Losses"

What to add: For any gene claimed to be absent or truncated in T. dohrnii relative to T. rubra:

Identify the syntenic block (conserved gene order) around the locus in T. rubra.

Locate the corresponding syntenic scaffolds in T. dohrnii using neighboring flanking genes.

Check whether the missing target gene lies directly on a scaffold boundary, gap (Ns), or break point. If it lies on a scaffold edge, classify it as "Unresolved/Ambiguous," not "Absent."

4. Develop a "Fragmentation Penalty Index" (Novel Computational Metric)

What makes it more novel: Create a simple, novel statistical metric or simulation script that estimates the empirical false-negative rate of your tBLASTn pipeline as a function of N50 and scaffold count. In silico fragment the T. rubra chromosome-level genome into mock assemblies matching T. dohrnii’s scaffold length distribution, re-run your tBLASTn pipeline on the mock dataset, and measure how many of your 48 target genes "disappear" purely due to artificial fragmentation.
